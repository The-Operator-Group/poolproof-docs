PoolProof supports multiple ways to log in — email and password, Google, or Microsoft — and gives you control over which methods your team is allowed to use. This page covers how each login option works, how to require company-wide single sign-on (SSO), how your customers log into the customer portal, and how multi-organization accounts work.

---

## Login Options for Your Team

Every PoolProof team member can log in using any of three methods:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Email and Password" icon="envelope">
    The standard login method. Enter your email address and password on the PoolProof login screen.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Sign in with Google" icon="google">
    Use your existing Google or Google Workspace account. Click **Sign in with Google** on the login screen — no separate PoolProof password needed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Sign in with Microsoft" icon="microsoft">
    Use your existing Microsoft or Microsoft 365 account. Click **Sign in with Microsoft** on the login screen — no separate PoolProof password needed.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Google and Microsoft login buttons appear on the PoolProof login screen by default. Your team members can start using them immediately — no setup required on their end.

## Why Use Google or Microsoft Login?

Using Google or Microsoft to sign in solves a common problem for growing pool service businesses: password management at scale.

When your team signs in through Google or Microsoft, they use an account they already access every day. There's no new credential to create, forget, or reset. Common problems — a technician locked out in a customer's driveway because they can't remember their password — go away.

Beyond convenience, Google and Microsoft handle security automatically:

- **Two-factor authentication** — managed at the identity provider level, without you configuring anything in PoolProof
- **Suspicious login detection** — Google and Microsoft flag and block unusual login attempts
- **Device management** — your existing corporate device policies apply automatically

### Automatic Lockout When Employees Leave

This is the most operationally important reason to use SSO for your team. If you use Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 and you've enabled **Require Social Login** in PoolProof, deactivating a departing employee's corporate account immediately locks them out of PoolProof as well.

No separate step. No hoping someone remembered to revoke access. The moment their Google or Microsoft account is deactivated, their PoolProof access is gone.

## Requiring SSO Company-Wide

By default, your team can use any login method. If you want to enforce consistency and take advantage of automatic lockout, you can require Google or Microsoft login for your entire organization.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Settings">
    In PoolProof, navigate to **Settings** in the main navigation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go to Security">
    Select the **Security** tab within Settings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable Require Social Login">
    Toggle on **Require Social Login**. Once enabled, email and password login is disabled company-wide. All team members must sign in through Google or Microsoft.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Before enabling Require Social Login, confirm that every team member has access to a Google or Microsoft account they can use to sign in. Team members without an eligible account will be locked out until the requirement is configured or they're provided an account.
</Warning>

## Customer Portal Login

Your customers access the PoolProof customer portal through a separate login flow from your team. Customers can authenticate using either:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Email and Password">
    Customers create a username and password when they first access the portal. They use these credentials on return visits. Standard account recovery via email is available.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Magic Link (Passwordless)">
    Customers enter their email address and receive a one-time login link — no password required. The link is valid for a single session. This is the simplest experience for customers who don't want to manage another password.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

The customer portal lets your customers view service history and past invoices, make payments, update their payment method on file, and request services — all without calling your office.

## Multi-Organization Support

If you manage more than one business entity in PoolProof — for example, if you own multiple pool service companies, or if a team member works across multiple organizations — PoolProof supports belonging to multiple organizations under a single login.

Users with access to multiple organizations can switch between them seamlessly from their account menu without logging out and back in. Each organization's data remains fully separate.

<Info>
  Multi-organization support is useful for franchise operations, business partnerships, or managers who oversee multiple service regions set up as separate PoolProof accounts.
</Info>

## Role-Based Access Control

Authentication (getting into PoolProof) is separate from authorization (what you can do once you're in). PoolProof's role-based access control system lets you assign each team member a role that defines exactly what they can see and do.

Five built-in roles cover most pool service team structures:

| Role | Access Level |
|---|---|
| **Owner** | Full, unrestricted access — cannot be modified or deleted |
| **Administrator** | Access to virtually everything except owner-level settings |
| **Regional Manager** | Customers, routes, quotes, work orders, and team oversight |
| **Sales** | Customer management and quoting — no route execution or billing |
| **Service Technician** | Assigned routes, service execution, readings, and notes |

You can also create custom roles from scratch using over 60 individual capabilities, scoped precisely to your team structure. For more detail, see the [Roles & Permissions](/getting-started/roles-and-permissions) page.
