# When a Customer Texts Back, the Right Tech Should See It

![](https://poolproof.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Issue-Tracking_edited-1024x683.jpg)

![](https://poolproof.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/wave-bg.svg)

You already send the “on the way” text. The tech taps to start driving, and the customer gets a friendly heads-up that someone’s headed their direction. It’s a small thing that quietly does a lot of work. It sets expectations, cuts down on no-access visits, and makes your business feel buttoned-up. 

But here’s the part that usually breaks: the customer texts back. 
“Great, the gate code is 4321.” 
“Can you also check the heater while you’re here?” 
“Actually, is it okay to leave the dog out, or should I bring her in?” 

That reply has to go somewhere. And on most systems, it goes to the office, not to the technician who is, at that very moment, two minutes from the driveway.

## The Reply That Never Reaches the Right Person

When a customer’s answer lands in a shared office inbox, a relay race begins. Someone has to notice the message, figure out which tech is headed to that address, and pass it along by text, by call, or by hoping the tech checks before they knock. Half the time the useful detail arrives after it mattered. 

The tech has already climbed the side fence, missed the heater request, or stood at a locked gate calling the office for a code the customer already sent. 

None of that is anyone’s fault. It’s just what happens when the message and the person who needs it are sitting in two different places. The information existed. It simply didn’t travel fast enough to be worth anything.

## Routing the Reply to the Tech Who’s Already on the Way

This is what intelligent message routing fixes. When a customer replies to an “on the way” text, PoolProof looks at who’s actually en route to that customer right now, the technician whose stop is currently driving or already in progress, and routes the reply straight to them. 

It shows up in the tech’s inbox on their phone, and they can answer the customer directly, in the moment, without the office ever touching it. 

It’s deliberately conservative about who it loops in. The en-route technician is added to the conversation, not swapped in. If a manager or office staffer is already on the thread, they stay on it. And the match is based on real route status, not a guess. 

It only points the message at the tech who is genuinely on the way to that address, from the moment the “on the way” notification goes out until the visit wraps up. No one outside that window gets pulled in.

## A Better Experience for the Customer

From the customer’s side, this feels like magic, but it’s really just relevance. They text “the side gate is unlocked,” and the person standing at the gate is the one who reads it. 

They ask a question and get an answer from someone who’s actually there, not a delayed reply from an office that has to chase down the field. 

That immediacy is what makes a service business feel reliable. The customer never wonders if their message disappeared into a void. 

They don’t repeat themselves on the next visit. And the small moments that used to turn into friction simply resolve themselves on the spot: a missed gate code, an unanswered “are you still coming?”

## Less Back-Office Relay, More Billable Time

Every one of those relays was costing you twice. It pulled an office staffer away from real work to play middleman, and it interrupted a technician’s day with a phone call that a single text could have handled. 

Multiply that across a full route, every day, and you’re paying real labor to move information that should have moved itself.

Cutting out the relay frees your office to focus on the work only they can do, and it keeps your techs moving instead of stopping to phone in. It also quietly protects your margins in the place margins usually leak: the callbacks and repeat trips that happen when a detail gets lost. 

A heater request answered while the tech is on site is revenue captured and a second trip avoided. That’s the whole PoolProof idea in miniature. Not just digitizing the back office, but removing the steps that were never worth a human’s time to begin with.

## Turning It On

It’s a single switch. In Settings → Inbox AI, turn on Intelligent Assignment, and PoolProof starts routing customer replies to the en-route technician automatically. There’s nothing to configure per tech. Once it’s on, it just works on your next route.

The one prerequisite is that SMS notifications are already running, since the whole flow starts with the “on the way” text. If you haven’t set that up yet, head to Settings → Notifications and Enable SMS Notifications first (this is also where your phone number gets connected). 

fWith that in place, the “on the way” message goes out, the reply comes back, and it lands with the right person, no relay required.
