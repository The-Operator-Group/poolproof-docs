## How PoolProof's Migration Works

PoolProof handles the entire migration for you before you sign or pay a dollar. That sequencing matters. Most platforms ask you to commit first and find out whether your data survived second. PoolProof inverts that: your data is loaded into a real, populated account and handed back to you so you can evaluate the platform with your own customers, routes, and service history already inside it.

You get a dedicated person throughout — from data import through configuration and team training. Most businesses go live in 30 days or less, and the migration service is included with your subscription at no extra charge (a $5,000 value).

<Steps>

### PoolProof pulls your data

Share exports from your current system with your dedicated migration specialist. PoolProof does the extraction work — you do not fill out a template or reshape columns.

### Your data is mapped and loaded

The migration team maps your records to PoolProof's data model and loads them into your account. Customers, routes, service history, equipment records, and billing data all land in the right places.

### You review a fully populated account

Log in and see your own business inside PoolProof before you make any commitment. Spot-check customers, verify service plans and pricing, and confirm routes look right. This is a real account — not a demo.

### PoolProof configures the platform for your workflow

Your dedicated specialist configures service plans, billing terms, checklist requirements, and automation settings to match how your business actually runs.

### Your team gets trained

PoolProof trains your technicians and office staff before go-live — not on the morning of it. Field staff learn the mobile app; office staff learn routing, billing, and customer management.

### You go live

Cut over at the end of a billing cycle and a service week. Run one service week in parallel if it helps your confidence, then you're fully on PoolProof.

</Steps>

## What Typically Doesn't Survive Any Migration

Four categories get lost quietly during software migrations in this industry. Knowing about them before you move means you can plan around them instead of discovering them at the worst possible moment.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Photos and attachments">
Photos do not live in a CSV. A spreadsheet export gives you a filename or a URL that stops resolving the day your old account closes. If you've been photographing equipment condition to protect yourself in disputes, that library is your liability defense — and it's the most commonly abandoned asset in a pool software migration. Before you cancel your old platform, download and back up every photo that matters to you.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Reading history as structured records">
Most platforms export service history to a spreadsheet, which is real and useful. But a flat spreadsheet of dates and numbers is not the same as a chemical reading tied to a specific visit, tied to a body of water, tied to the equipment on that pad. Getting the file is easy. Getting it to arrive as a fully structured, queryable service record on the other side is the actual work — and it depends heavily on the quality and format of what your old system exports.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Informal agreements and verbal notes">
The "don't service on Thursdays" arrangement. The verbal approval on a repair. The access instructions that only one technician knows. Migration is an ideal moment to write these things down for the first time. Anything that isn't in your system today won't survive the move.
</Accordion>

See the payment method section below — this one deserves its own warning.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

## Payment Method Handling

<Warning>
Your customers' credit cards and bank accounts are **not stored in your pool service software** — they are vaulted with the payment processor. Switching software platforms often means switching payment processors, and card tokens do not automatically transfer between processors.

If a vault-to-vault transfer is not available, you will need to re-collect payment authorization from every autopay customer before your next billing run. Plan for this deliberately — running a re-authorization campaign mid-billing cycle is the most disruptive self-inflicted wound in a pool software migration.

Ask PoolProof specifically about payment processor continuity as part of your migration conversation.
</Warning>

When autopay authorizations must be re-collected, PoolProof helps you run that campaign cleanly — with customer communications, a re-authorization link, and tracking so you know exactly which accounts still need a payment method before billing runs.
